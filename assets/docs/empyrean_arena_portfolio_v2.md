## Overview

Empyrean Arena is a 1v1 tabletop action game built on the pacing of competitive fighting games and the spectacle of shonen anime tournament arcs. Players step into structured tournament bouts as one of two Protagonist archetypes, resolving combat through a dice-and-card system built around two core resources: Tempo, which governs timing and initiative, and Momentum, which builds through exchanges and pays off in decisive moments.

This started as a personal passion project, something I was building on my own before it had any academic backing. My Minor Skilled program gave it real stakes: a structured timeline, external feedback, and the push to take an early prototype and turn it into a complete, print-ready starter kit, complete with a designed rules manual, a full card set with final art, player sheets, and a sample battle map. The result is the most complete expression of my design philosophy to date: fast-paced, snappy combat that makes players feel powerful.

Development continues beyond the program.

## My Role

Sole designer, end to end. I owned every layer of the project:

- Game design: combat mechanics, resource systems, archetype design, card text
- Systems research: studying resolution systems and comparable products to ground design decisions
- Playtesting: running sessions, collecting data, and translating results into concrete revisions
- UI/UX and visual design: building the full manual and card layouts in Figma from the ground up, including a custom color palette, typography system, and modular components designed for player-facing clarity, alongside a companion app prototype built to extend that same design language digitally
- Documentation: rules writing, card copy conventions, and a full process report

Two outside reviewers gave feedback at key milestones, but every design decision, mechanic, and line of card text is mine. I'm happy to share the full process, research, and supporting documentation with anyone interested in how I work or how I arrived at the final product.

## Process

The project followed a Double Diamond structure across four phases.

**Discover.** This phase was research-heavy: I built out a deep research logbook covering TTRPG systems design theory and resolution mechanics from existing published games, which grounded every design decision that came after. Early feedback also forced a necessary scope cut, narrowing an overly ambitious original vision down to a focused core combat loop and two archetypes. The phase ended with a clear design mantra that guided the rest of the project: fast-paced, snappy combat power-fantasy.

**Define.** I took the research and scope from Discover and turned it into a testable first ruleset, alongside a set of measurable design goals I could evaluate future playtests against. This phase sharpened my ability to translate research and creative intent into concrete, testable design criteria, a skill I leaned on for the rest of the project.

**Develop.** The longest phase, built around three major in-person playtest cycles with real players. Each cycle followed the same loop: run and organize a structured session, collect data, identify the specific problem, and revise the mechanic causing it. A recurring theme throughout was disciplined scope control: cutting mechanics that let one stat or system dominate, in favor of cleaner, more elegant solutions.

**Deliver.** A final refinement pass polished the ruleset, closed edge cases, and prepared everything for print. In parallel, I built the complete manual in Figma, replaced placeholder art with final assets, and finished a companion app prototype. The phase closed with a full report documenting the entire design process.

Every major revision was driven by playtest data, not intuition.

## Key Highlights

- **A complete, print-ready starter kit**: manual, full card set with final art, player sheets, and a sample battle map, taken from rough prototype to finished product in one semester.
- **A resolution system built from scratch**: refined across three full playtest cycles with real players.
- **Disciplined scope management**: repeated, deliberate cuts to mechanics and content that didn't serve the core mantra, rather than letting the game accumulate untested complexity.
- **Data-driven iteration**: every version change traces back to specific tally data and observed play problems, not gut feeling.
- **A full visual identity**: custom color palette, typography, and modular Figma components built to match the tone of the game before a single page of the manual was laid out.
- **A companion app prototype**: an early proof of concept for digitizing parts of play, built from a Figma wireframe into working HTML/CSS.
